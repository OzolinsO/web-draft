Under the hood

Complexity handled where nobody has to see it

Your customer sees a lower bill. You see a clean fleet overview. Between the two runs a continuous cycle of measurement, forecasting and control, executing at every site without anyone logging in.

01

Data collection

The on-site controller reads inverters, meters, batteries, heat pumps and chargers over their native protocols, at the resolution both optimisation and diagnostics require.

02

Transfer to cloud

Data is buffered locally and synchronised to the platform over an encrypted link. Connectivity gaps cost you no data, and the site keeps operating while the link is down.

03

Consumption forecasting

Historical patterns predict what the site will consume, hour by hour. The optimiser then plans against expected demand rather than reacting to load after it appears.

04

External data enrichment

Forecasts combine with hourly power prices, weather data and grid signals. That external context determines what the correct decision at any given hour actually is.

05

Battery scenario building

The platform calculates the charge and discharge schedule with the best financial outcome for the coming horizon, then revises it as prices and site conditions move.

06

Fault detection

Live readings are checked against expected behaviour and manufacturer limits. Any deviation raises an alert naming the affected asset and the probable cause.

07

Predictive analysis

Long-term trends expose slow degradation that no single reading reveals — falling capacity, rising operating temperatures, declining string output. Intervention gets planned instead of improvised.

08

Command execution

Decisions are written back to the equipment as setpoints and control commands. Local logic keeps executing safely if the cloud connection drops mid-schedule.

Do I have to change the equipment I install?

No. HeloGrid Edge is vendor agnostic and works with common inverters, batteries, heat pumps and meters. Your suppliers, margins and installation practice stay as they are.

Who owns the customer relationship?

You do. The service is delivered under your business, your contract and your pricing. We supply the platform behind it and stay out of the customer relationship.

Who decides what the customer pays monthly?

You do. The price reflects your supervision, maintenance and on-site work. It is your commercial decision, based on your market, your cost base and your competition.

How long does commissioning take?

The controller is installed in the panel, connected to the site equipment, and the assets are detected and configured. [FIGURE: typical commissioning duration]

What happens if the internet connection drops?

The site keeps running. Optimisation, protection and grid compliance execute locally on the device. Data is buffered and synchronised once the link returns, with nothing lost.

Does this help with manufacturer warranty claims?

Yes. Many suppliers require documented online supervision. Continuous logging keeps that condition satisfied and preserves the operating history you need when equipment fails inside its warranty term.

Can I manage all my sites in one place?

Yes. Every installation appears in a single fleet view with status, alerts and performance. One person supervises a portfolio instead of opening individual manufacturer portals.

What size of installation does it suit?

Residential through to commercial sites. The same platform manages a single-battery home and a multi-asset business site. [FIGURE: supported capacity range]

How does this protect me from price competition?

Equipment and installation increasingly get compared on price alone. A managed service is judged on outcomes — savings, uptime, response time — which is far harder to undercut.
